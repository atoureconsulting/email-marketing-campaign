# Email Rules — Baba Toure / AToure Management & Consulting

> Applied to every email written for this client, on top of the global rules in Context/Marketing/copy-rules.md.

---

## Structure Defaults

- **Opening:** Jarring statement, real campaign hook, or direct challenge. Never a generic greeting or "I hope this finds you well."
- **First line:** Start with the subscriber's first name variable. Always.
- **Body:** One core idea per email. No multiple CTAs. No topic-switching mid-email.
- **Paragraphs:** 2-4 sentences maximum. One idea per paragraph.
- **Closing:** Restate core message > CTA (soft and direct) > sign-off
- **Sign-off:** Baba Toure / AToure Management & Consulting / atoureconsulting.com

---

## CTA Rules

- Soft and direct. Never aggressive. Never "BUY NOW" or urgency-manufactured pressure.
- Preferred forms:
  - "Would you be open to a 20-minute call this week?"
  - "Reply and let me know what you're working on."
  - "If this is relevant, [Calendly link] — pick a time that works."
- Calendly link: add once account is live. Use natural lowercase link text.
- One CTA per email. Never stack two asks.

---

## Audience-Specific Rules

**For brand marketing leads:**
- Lead with the outcome for their campaign, not AToure's credentials
- Credits are proof, not biography — drop them in context, not as a CV
- Respect that they have seen agency pitches before. Don't sound like one.

**For creators:**
- More direct and human
- What does representation actually look like day to day? That's the answer they want
- Don't oversell — creators have been burned before

**For African market / event clients:**
- The on-the-ground angle is the differentiator — name it specifically
- Government contacts and logistics capacity are real and unique. Use them as proof, not as boasts.

---

## Proof Rules (strict)

- Use only the verified campaign credits listed in knowledge.md
- Do not invent client quotes, testimonials, or results
- Do not construct composite stories or "representative" examples
- If proof is thin for a given angle, write around it with specifics: the market, the challenge, the capability — not a fake story

## What This Audience Responds To

- Specificity over volume: one specific campaign credit beats a long list of vague claims
- Access framing: the question they're always asking is "can you actually deliver this?" Answer that directly
- Efficiency: brand leads are busy. Short, clear, one ask
- Earned authority: the voice should feel like someone who has been in those rooms, not someone describing what those rooms are like

---

## What to Avoid

- Any language that sounds like a platform or marketplace
- Urgency tactics (scarcity, countdown timers, FOMO) — inconsistent with premium positioning
- Impersonal, broadcast-corporate tone
- Anything that requires the reader to forward, share, or tag to get value
- Opening with a question as the very first word (overused pattern)
