# Voice Context — Baba Toure / AToure Management & Consulting

> Loaded alongside knowledge.md when writing any copy for this client.

---

## Core Voice Identity

Baba writes and speaks with the quiet confidence of someone who doesn't need to perform authority — because the network, the credits, and the room he's been in already do that. Copy should feel composed and premium without being cold. Human without being casual. Direct without being blunt to the point of feeling transactional.

The voice is built on access and trust, not hype. The work is high-stakes (creators, brands, governments, African markets) so the tone reflects that weight.

---

## Tone by Context

### Pitch / Outreach emails
- Formal structure. Short paragraphs.
- Lead with the credit or the outcome, not the biography.
- Make the ask clear. Don't bury it.
- No waffle, no padding. Every sentence earns its place.

### Follow-up / Nurture emails
- Shorter than the initial outreach.
- Acknowledges the relationship without being sycophantic.
- Confident — not chasing, not apologising for following up.
- Ends with a soft open door.

### Broadcast / List emails
- More human than a pitch. Still composed.
- Can reference something real (a campaign, a moment, a thought) as the hook.
- One idea per email. No everything-at-once.

### Conversational (replies, short messages)
- Relaxed but not loose.
- Faith references ("by God's grace") feel natural here.
- Genuine interest in the other person. Not performing warmth.

## Language Rules

**Use:**
- "by God's grace" — natural aside, not a marketing hook
- "on the ground" — signals real operational presence
- "the right fit" — central to how AToure works
- "end to end" — signals full accountability
- "we handle it" / "we take care of it" — reassurance without overselling
- "access" — the real product being offered
- First name of recipient when available

**Never use:**
- "influencer" — always "creator"
- "going viral" / "crush it" / "killing it"
- "synergy," "solutions," "leverage," "utilise," "streamline," "elevate," "empower"
- "game-changer" or any phrase that swaps a specific outcome for a vague superlative
- Generic agency language that could come from any firm
- Em dashes (hard rule across all copy)
- Any phrase from the banned AI list in copy-rules.md

**Profanity:** None, ever.

**Faith:** Natural integration only. "By God's grace" is the clearest example. Not a sales tool. Never used to close or persuade — only as a genuine expression of how Baba speaks.

## What Makes This Voice Distinct

1. **The network is earned, not bought.** Copy should reflect years of relationship-building, not a company that spun up last year with a database. "Built over years" is true and it matters.

2. **Premium through restraint.** The brand doesn't oversell. Confidence doesn't need exclamation marks. Proof doesn't need adjectives.

3. **Africa is not a buzzword here.** This is family, contacts, years of presence. Copy treats African market access as a specific, serious, operational capability — not a trend angle.

4. **Bilingual background.** Precision in word choice. No filler. English is considered, not automatic.

## Red Flags in Drafted Copy

If a draft contains any of the following, revise before sending:

- Three consecutive short sentences followed by a big-claim fourth
- Any rhetorical question stack (more than one in a row)
- "Not just X but Y" construction used more than once
- Any banned phrase from copy-rules.md
- A fabricated testimonial, result, or story
- Language that sounds like a SaaS startup rather than a premium operator
- Anything that sounds like it was written for a different continent's market without considering the UK/Africa audience split
